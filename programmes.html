Beginner

AI for Everyday Problem Solving

Use AI to understand, check and organise your own learning, and finish with a study companion you built yourself. No coding needed to start.

The project, piece by piece

  1. Study briefDay 1
  2. Checked notebookDay 2

Day01

How generative AI works, and how to ask it well

Why AI can sound certain and still be wrong. Students see how a chatbot predicts its answers, then practise asking precise questions.

Gemini
What they build

A study brief: their goal, exam date and weak topics, plus a prompt they can reuse.

Try it at home: Ask AI to explain one topic at three levels (Class 6, Class 10, expert) and note one claim you would need to check.

Day02

Grounding AI in sources you trust

Make AI answer from your own chapter material, then check every important claim. What "grounding" means, and how to judge a source.

GeminiGemini Notebook
What they build

A study notebook built from their own chapter notes. They ask it five questions and check each answer against the page it quotes.

Try it at home: Add notes for a second chapter and repeat the five-question check.

Day03

Build your personal AI tutor

Turn AI from an answer machine into a tutor that makes you think. Why retrieval practice works, and how to use AI without handing over the thinking.

Gemini
What they build

A personal tutor that quizzes them instead of telling them, and a 10-question revision quiz they have checked for mistakes.

Try it at home: Use your tutor for 15 minutes on a new topic. Note your score and one thing it got wrong.

Day04

Let AI handle the routine, safely

Connect AI to everyday apps while deciding exactly what it may see and do. Triggers, permissions, and keeping a human in the loop.

GeminiGoogle CalendarGoogle Sheets
What they build

A revision plan in Google Calendar, a daily quiz reminder and a study log.

Try it at home: Log each study session this week. Your own data can replace the sample on Day 5.

Day05

Your first steps in Python

Why code? Exact counts and charts where AI only estimates. Students install Python and run the same script in VS Code and in Jupyter.

PythonVS CodeJupyter
What they build

A Python tracker that charts their study log: the last piece of the AI Study Companion.

Try it at home: Ask AI to suggest one more chart for your tracker and describe it in plain English.

Final day

Demo Day

A buffer day. Anyone who is behind catches up first. Then every student demonstrates their AI Study Companion to parents and teachers in three minutes.

What they build

The complete AI Study Companion, presented and explained.

Building AI Solutions with Python

Use AI to write, organise, debug and test real Python code, and finish with an application you built and can explain line by line.

  2. Quiz engineDay 2

The app runs on the student's own laptop and needs no API key or paid account. Students use AI to write and debug the code, not inside the app.

Day01

How AI writes code, and how to check it

Treat AI-written code as a draft to verify, not an answer to trust. Why code can look right and still fail, and how to ask AI for code well.

GeminiVS CodePython
What they build

A real Python project set up in VS Code, a question bank built from their notes and checked against sources, and code that loads it.

Try it at home: Add 10 questions for a second chapter and run the checks again.

Day02

From one script to modules

Break a program into small parts that can be understood, reused and tested. Design the pieces first, then direct AI to write them, then review its code.

GeminiPython modules
What they build

A working quiz program, split into modules, that records every attempt.

Try it at home: Take three quizzes before the next class. Your attempts can replace the sample data.

Day03

Debugging with AI, and proving it works

Read error messages, fix them with AI's help, and prove each fix with automated tests. The day starts from a file with five planted bugs.

VS Code debuggerpytestpandas
What they build

A tested analysis that charts each student's weakest topics.

Try it at home: Break your code in two ways and ask AI to find the cause without telling it.

Day04

How AI finds evidence

Open the black box behind AI search: how machines measure similarity, why some AI tools can cite their sources, and how to measure whether search actually works.

scikit-learnGemini Notebook
What they build

A "revise from here" feature: for each wrong answer, the app shows the paragraph in their notes to revise, and students measure how often it is right.

Try it at home: Find a question where search fails, change how the notes are split, and measure again.

Day05

Ship it

Turn weak topics into a revision plan, bring every module together into one app, and rehearse a three-minute demo.

PythonGoogle Calendar
What they build

The finished Exam Prep Coach: it quizzes, finds weak topics, shows what to revise and adds a revision plan to Google Calendar.

Try it at home: Use the Coach for a week, add one feature you want, and debug it with AI.

Final day

Demo Day

A buffer day. Anyone behind catches up first. Then every student demonstrates Exam Prep Coach, walks through one module and explains one bug they fixed.

Exam Prep Coach, tested, documented and demonstrated.

Which one is right?

Start where the student is.

Choose Beginner if
  • The student has never written code.
  • They want to use AI well for their own studies, in any subject.
  • They are in any stream: science, commerce or humanities.
Choose Advanced if
  • They have finished Beginner, or can already run a Python script.
  • They want to build software, not just use apps.
  • They are comfortable installing tools on their own laptop.

What students need

A laptop, a Google account and their own notes.

  • A laptop they can install software on. Windows or macOS works best.
  • A Google account, set up with a parent's consent.
  • Notes for one chapter of a subject they are studying. The project is built around their own material.
  • Curiosity. Questions are the point.
